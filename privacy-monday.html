Copy Item to Board for monday.com

Last updated 4 November 2026

This page says what the app does with your data in plain terms. Where the app cannot prove something, it says so.

The short version. The app copies an item from one board to another inside your own monday.com account. It reads the item and writes the copy using your permissions. It runs on monday.com's own hosting (monday code), has no database, and sends nothing to any other company.

What the app reads

Only what is needed to make the copy, and only for the item you chose: its name, its column values, its subitems, its updates and replies (with their authors and dates), and the files attached to them. It also reads the list of boards you can see, and the columns and groups of the source and target boards, so it can match them.

The app asks monday.com for these permissions (scopes) and no others:

  • boards:read and boards:write — read the source item and boards; create the copy.
  • updates:read and updates:write — read the item's updates; write them on the copy.
  • assets:read — read the files, so the copy can point at the same files.

It asks for no access to users, workspaces, or account settings. Everything it does is limited by what the person who clicked can already see and do in monday.com.

Where the work happens

When you copy by hand, in your browser: the app's window talks to monday.com's API with your session, and no server of ours is involved.

When an automation copies an item, on a small server hosted by monday.com's own platform (monday code). monday.com sends it a signed request; the server checks the signature, makes the copy through monday.com's API with a short-lived token that belongs to that run (it expires about 30 minutes after it is issued), and reports the result back to monday.com. We do not run any other server, and nothing is sent to any other company.

What the app keeps

No database, no copy of your boards, no stored tokens, no analytics.

  • The items and updates themselves exist only in your monday.com account. The app passes them from one board to another and keeps nothing.
  • For automations, the server remembers the identifier of each run it has already started, for one hour and in memory only, so that a repeated request cannot create a second copy. It is a random id, not your data. The short-lived token travels to the copy step through monday code's own queue and is used for that one run.
  • Server logs (kept by monday code) contain run identifiers, item and board numbers, counts such as "3 subitems, 5 updates", and error messages. They do not contain the text of your items or updates.
  • In your browser, the window remembers your last three target boards and groups (their names and ids) so you can pick them again. This stays in your browser's local storage and never leaves it. Clearing your browser data removes it.

Where it is stored

Your data stays where monday.com keeps your account. The copy is created in the same account, so data residency does not change. The automation server runs on monday.com's hosting in the EU region.

What the app sends anywhere else

Nothing outside monday.com. The app does not call third-party services, does not load scripts or fonts from third-party hosts, and has no analytics or error-reporting service. Its only network traffic goes to monday.com (its API, its hosting, and the address monday.com gives it to report a run's result).

What the copy shows

Because monday.com's API writes updates as the person who runs the copy, each copied update starts with a line such as "Originally posted by Ana on 2026-10-30 14:05 UTC", so the history stays readable. The last update on the copy says where it was copied from and lists anything that could not be copied (for example, a column that has no match on the target board). The original item is never changed.

Personal data

The app is not designed to collect personal data. It does move the names of the people who wrote updates, as part of the history it copies, from one board of your account to another. That data stays in your account. Softyways does not receive, store, or process data from your account, and is neither a data controller nor a data processor for it.

How to delete it

There is nothing of ours to delete. Uninstalling the app removes its access to your account. The copies it created are ordinary items on your boards; delete them as you would any other item.

Changes to this policy

If the app's behavior changes, this page changes with it and the date above moves. If a future version asked for a new permission, it would be visible on the app's monday.com listing.
