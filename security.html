Security policy

Softyways apps for Atlassian Cloud

Last updated 2 September 2026

This page describes how Softyways builds, ships and maintains its apps, and what security properties those apps have. It is a companion to the privacy policies, not a replacement for them: the privacy policies say what data an app touches, this page says how the app and the practice around it are secured.

Every claim here is meant to be checkable against each app's Forge manifest and its published listing, not taken on trust. Where a control does not exist, this page says so plainly rather than implying it.

The short version. Both apps run entirely on Atlassian Forge, on Atlassian's own infrastructure. Softyways operates no servers, holds no copy of customer data, and has no console through which it could read one. Neither app sends anything to a third party. Dependencies are pinned and audited on every release. Vulnerability reports go to security@softyways.com.

Where the apps run

Both apps are Atlassian Forge apps. They are hosted and executed by Atlassian, inside Atlassian's cloud, under the platform's own tenancy isolation, authentication and network controls. Softyways does not run application servers, does not operate a database, and does not maintain infrastructure that holds customer data.

This is a deliberate architectural choice rather than a convenience. It means the classes of risk that dominate vendor breaches — an exposed database, a leaked server credential, an unpatched host, a misconfigured storage bucket — do not have a place to occur on our side, because there is no such component.

What each app can reach

A Forge app can only do what its manifest declares. The declarations below are visible to any reviewer and to any administrator installing the app.

  Mermaid SVG Diagrams for Confluence Permission Review for Jira
Atlassian API scopes None. scopes is an empty list read:jira-work, read:jira-user, storage:app — all read-only except its own storage
Write access to your site None None. No write scope is requested, so the app cannot change a permission, only report on it
Back end None. No resolver, no function, no web trigger A Forge resolver function, executed by Atlassian
Data stored by the app Nothing. Diagram text is a macro parameter, saved by Confluence inside your page as your content A review baseline, in Forge storage, inside your Atlassian tenancy
External network access None. The manifest declares no permissions.external None. The manifest declares no permissions.external
Third-party services None. No analytics, no error reporting, no sub-processors None. No analytics, no error reporting, no sub-processors

Because neither app declares any external permission, no data can leave Atlassian's boundary at runtime — the platform enforces this, not our good intentions. This is the technical property the Runs on Atlassian designation is based on.

One declaration is worth naming explicitly, because a reader of the manifest will see it: the Confluence app declares content.styles: 'unsafe-inline'. Mermaid emits a <style> block inside the SVG it generates, and without that declaration the diagram renders unstyled. It applies to the app's own rendered output. No other permission of any kind is declared.

Data handling

The governing principle is that an app should never hold data it does not need. In practice that means the Confluence app holds nothing at all, and the Jira app holds only the small baseline it needs in order to answer the question “what changed since the last review?” — inside the customer's own Atlassian tenancy, never in a Softyways system.

Softyways staff cannot read customer data through an administrative interface, because no such interface exists. There is no export, no support console, and no copy of a customer's content on any machine we control. Support is conducted by email, on information a customer chooses to send us.

Encryption

In transit. All traffic between a browser and an app is served over TLS by Atlassian. Neither app opens a network connection of its own, so there is no additional transport for us to secure or to get wrong.

At rest. The Confluence app stores nothing, so there is nothing of ours at rest. The Jira app's baseline is held in Forge storage, which is encrypted at rest by the Atlassian platform under Atlassian's own key management. Softyways does not operate its own encryption keys, because it operates no storage.

We claim no cryptography of our own design. Neither app implements custom encryption, and neither should: the platform's controls are stronger than anything a small vendor would hand-roll.

Development and change management

  • All source is held in private version control, with a full history of every change.
  • Releases are versioned and deployed through the Forge CLI to Atlassian, which is the only deployment path that exists. There is no server to log into and no configuration to drift.
  • Changes are reviewed before release, and the manifest — the file that determines everything an app is permitted to do — is reviewed on every change, specifically for new scopes or new permissions.
  • A change that would add a scope or an external permission is treated as a material change: it becomes visible on the Marketplace listing and requires administrator consent at upgrade time.
  • Development accounts used to publish the apps are protected with two-factor authentication.

Dependency and vulnerability management

Both apps carry a deliberately small dependency tree, because every dependency is an attack surface that has to be watched.

  • Dependency versions are pinned, not floating, so a build cannot silently pull new code.
  • npm audit is run against the full dependency tree before every release. A release does not ship with a known unresolved vulnerability in a production dependency.
  • As of 2 September 2026 both apps report zero known vulnerabilities. The Confluence app's production dependencies are mermaid 11.16.1 and @forge/bridge 4.5.3.
  • Runtime and platform patching is performed by Atlassian as part of Forge; apps declare a supported Node.js runtime and are kept on a supported version.
  • Static analysis is limited to dependency composition analysis and linting. We do not claim to run commercial SAST or DAST tooling, and do not imply coverage we do not have.

Reporting a vulnerability

Reports are welcome, including from researchers with no commercial relationship with us, and will not be met with legal threats for good-faith testing.

Send reports to security@softyways.com. Please include enough detail to reproduce the issue, and the app and version affected.

  • We aim to acknowledge a report within 5 business days.
  • We aim to give an initial assessment, with a severity and an intended course of action, within 15 business days.
  • Where a fix is required, it is released as promptly as the severity warrants, and reporters are told when it ships.
  • Where an incident affects customer data, affected customers and Atlassian are notified, in line with Atlassian Marketplace partner obligations.

Softyways does not currently operate a paid bug bounty. Credit is offered to reporters who want it.

Certifications and compliance

Softyways holds no security certifications. We are not SOC 2 audited and not ISO 27001 certified, and we would rather say so than let a reader assume otherwise.

What we rely on instead is the Forge platform: the apps inherit the certifications, controls and compliance posture of the Atlassian cloud they run inside, precisely because they hold no data and run no infrastructure outside it. Atlassian publishes its own certifications and its Trust Center, and they apply to the environment our apps execute in.

On data protection: the apps process no personal data on Softyways systems, because Softyways operates no systems that receive it. Softyways is established in the European Union and applies the GDPR to the limited contact data it holds — that is, the email addresses of people who write to us.

Changes to this policy

If the apps' behaviour or our practice changes, this page changes with it and the date above moves. The claims here are tied to the manifests: if a future version asked for a scope or an external permission, that would be visible on the app's Marketplace listing before it was visible here.
